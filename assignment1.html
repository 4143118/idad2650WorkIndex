Scenario

I chose Musical Toy for Toddlers as my scenario. I think this scenario focuses on providing a way for children whose language skills are still developing to explore music. I think traditional digital Musical Instruments usually rely on textual descriptions, professional terms and complex control methods, which are somewhat obscure for someone who has never learned music knowledge, let alone young children. Therefore, this Scenario requires that musical instruments communicate more in a simple and easy-to-understand way, or even directly use voice guidance. This understanding enables my project to focus on direct exploration and immediate comprehension, while requiring the design to be childlike, accessible, and give users the space to freely try.

Brief Synopsis

This project is a browser-based interactive synthesizer for kids. It enables users to directly trigger different sounds through a few simple buttons, such as the sounds of musical instruments and the chirping of animals. Each button uses intuitive icons instead of text descriptions, and is accompanied by simple volume control and voice guidance. The overall interface adopts a childlike visual language, and enabling young children to independently explore different sounds and create simple music without having music knowledge or strong reading skills.

Purpose

The aim of this project is to enable young children to explore music independently through games and sounds. It should provide young children with a music environment that is easy to understand and participate in, enabling them to come into contact with and experience music even without musical knowledge or strong language skills. Users can discover the relationships between different sounds through their own attempts, gradually understand how their operations affect the sounds, and eventually arrange a piece of music that belongs to them. The project hopes to use a simple synthesizer to arouse children's interest in music.

Worth

1. Accessibility, it means reducing the barriers that language proficiency and musical knowledge pose to young children's use of digital musical instruments. As the language skills of the target users are still developing, the design should help users understand the interface through intuitive visual elements, simple interactions and auditory feedback, enabling young children to explore with less reliance on adult assistance.

2. Playfulness. I hope this project is close to a toy that can be freely explored rather than a professional music tool that requires learning and correct operation. The design should make the interactive process easy and interesting, and create an experience suitable for young children through visuals and sounds. Users do not need to complete a certain task, instead, they can naturally come into contact with and explore sounds through play.

3. Encouraging, it means encouraging young children to actively try different operations and discover the possible sound results they may produce. Design should provide positive feedback and encourage users to explore. This enables users to build an understanding of the relationship between sound and interaction through continuous attempts, and discover the sounds and combinations they are interested in during the exploration process.

Framing

1. In terms of visual appeal, the project will adopt a cute and childlike visual style, drawing on the visual language of children's picture books. It will create a friendly interface atmosphere through simple shapes and easily recognizable images, making it closer to a children's toy.

2. For the interaction aspect, the project will strive to use simple click and drag interactions, allowing users to trigger and control sounds through direct operations, while avoiding overly complex or diverse interaction methods. The number of interaction methods will also be limited, enabling users to quickly understand the basic usage of the interface.

3. For the information principle, the project will reduce its reliance on text messages and mainly convey information through intuitive icons and voice guidance. Icons are used to help users identify different buttons and functions, while voice guidance is used to provide simple operation prompts, enabling young children whose language skills are still developing to understand the interface.

4. Feedback. Users' operations will receive immediate audio and visual feedback. When users trigger sounds or attempt to combine different sounds, the interface responds through animations, sound changes or simple encouraging voices. It allows users to understand that their operations have produced results and encouraging them to continue exploring.

Interaction design analysis

Example 1: Palindrum

What attracts me most about Palindrum is that it directly integrates the concept of palindrome into its interface and music functions. The interface as a whole adopts a left-right mirror design, with all the text on the right presented in a reversed form. This visual "reversal" is not merely decoration but forms a direct connection with the reverse playback function of the instrument. After users see the mirror structure of the interface, they can naturally associate the visual concept of "reversal" with the function of sound being played in reverse, without the need for additional explanations. This design makes me feel that there is a very interesting and clear mapping between its concept, visual features and functions. The inspiration this UI brought to my project is that I hope my synthesizer can also create an intuitive mapping between visual elements and sound functions. Rather than simply using text to explain the function of each button, I hope the Icons themselves can help users understand the corresponding sounds or operations, allowing young children to establish connections through "what they see" and "what they hear", thereby reducing their reliance on language explanations.

Example 2: OTS-01

This synthesizer provides visual feedback for the user's control through real-time waveform display. When the user operates the keyboard or adjusts the synthesizer parameters, the waveform above will change in real time with the currently generated sound, allowing the user not only to hear the changes in the sound but also to directly observe them. This visual feedback establishes an immediate connection between user operations, sound results and interface changes, transforming originally invisible sounds into observable visual information. For my project, this feedback approach made me realize that voice-based interactions do not necessarily have to rely solely on the voice itself to reflect the user's operations. My synthesizer is designed for young children with limited language skills, so I also hope to provide immediate and easily understandable visual feedback. However, compared with this UI, I won't use complex waveforms or spectra to represent sounds, as this information might be hard for young children to understand. I prefer to use simple animations, button changes or other visual responses with a childlike touch, allowing users to intuitively feel that their operations are generating sounds and further encouraging them to keep exploring.

Example 3: Karaoke Night

Karaoke Night distinguishes different instruments and vocals through various images. For instance, the icons of drums, guitars and keyboards in the instrument area can directly correspond to different sounds. While the vocal area uses patterns such as elephants, socks and bananas that have no direct connection to represent different sounds. Although some vocal icons do not have a very direct connection with their corresponding sounds, they use simple and recognizable graphics. At the same time, the unified illustration style also gives the entire interface a distinct childlike charm. This kind of information design has a very direct inspiration for my project. My synthesizer will also reduce the text information and use icons as the main information carrier. For sounds that can be directly associated with functions, such as drums or pianos, I will use icons with clear mapping. For sounds that are difficult to express directly through images, patterns that are more childlike and visually recognizable can be used instead of forcibly establishing an accurate semantic connection between the icons and the sounds. This way, not only can children's reliance on words be reduced, but also the interface can be made more like a musical toy through a unified visual style.

Example 4: Pinkalicous & Peterrific

This game designs the interface as a world with its own environment and characters. The forest, flowers, trees, characters and various decorative elements together form a story-like scene, making users feel that they are entering a children's world belonging to this work. Even if users have not engaged in any interaction yet, these visual elements have already conveyed the atmosphere, characters and imagination of this world. The impact this had on my project was that I began to realize that characterisation doesn't necessarily come from the buttons and icons themselves, it can also come from the worldview of the entire interface. Therefore, I hope my synthesizer is not just a "cute music tool", but can be used to build a small music world of my own. As a result, visual design not only undertakes the function of information transmission, but also can become a part of the project experience and story sense.
